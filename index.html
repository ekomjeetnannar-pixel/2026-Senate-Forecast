    How the forecast works, in plain English

    Step 1: Fundamentals. Start with how the state usually votes. The forecast takes 60% of the 2024 presidential margin and 40% of the state's last Senate margin, then adds 3 points for a senator running for reelection.

    fundamentals = 0.6 × 2024 pres margin + 0.4 × last Senate margin ± 3 (incumbent running)

    Step 2: Polls. Average the state's polls. The more polls there are, the more they count. With 3 polls, polls and fundamentals count equally. With 18 polls (Texas), polls count for 86%.

    forecast = fundamentals × 3/(n+3) + poll average × n/(n+3)

    Step 3: Uncertainty. Each race gets a base error of 5 points, more when there are few polls, plus a 3 point national swing that moves every race together. That spread turns a margin into a win chance.

    Example, Georgia: 0.6 × (−2.2) + 0.4 × (−1.8) + 3 for Ossoff = D+1.0 fundamentals. Two polls averaging D+8 get 40% weight, giving a D+3.8 forecast and a 72% chance for Ossoff. He won by 18.

    What went wrong

    The national environment. The forecast assumed no national shift, but results ran about points more Democratic than forecast on average. Because fundamentals lean on 2024, a year Republicans did well, the model anchored red states too far right. Races with few polls (Kansas, South Carolina, Alabama) missed the most, since fundamentals did most of the work there.
